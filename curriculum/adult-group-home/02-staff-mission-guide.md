# Rift Signal: staff mission guide
Best Polity LLC · Adult group-home edition · Draft 1

## Start here
This guide supports the frozen grid-menu DVD approved by the owner. It is for adults who choose to take part. Read the [rationale and crosswalk](01-rationale-and-standards.md) for the framework, claim limits, and sources. The disc remains unchanged.

Use the fifteen lessons flexibly. One episode plus a short real-world activity is a useful starting invitation; it is not a fixed session length. The approximately 44 minutes of main-path audio excludes scanning, responses, breaks, discussion, and activities. Do not promise a participant that a lesson must finish in a set number of minutes.

The **Mission Book belongs to the participant**. They can choose what goes in it and who sees it. Staff notes are a separate record.

## Before the first invitation
- Ask whether the person wants to try the story, the book, both, or another activity.
- Check the person's usual communication and access arrangements with them and, where needed, their support team. Establish how they indicate yes, no, help, wait, stop, and a changed choice; do not invent signals for them.
- Test the remote or switch connection with this player. Ordinary DVD compatibility does not establish compatibility with a particular access switch.
- Check sound, viewing position, lighting, picture visibility, and the person's access to AAC or other communication tools. Offer descriptions or adapted materials when needed.
- Have the relevant book pages and a small number of materials ready. Use large objects or representations suitable for the person; manipulating small pieces is never required.
- Ask before photographing, recording, displaying, or sharing anyone or their home. A photograph of a chosen object or place can avoid including other people. A drawing or description is equally usable.
- Use ordinary adult language. Read aloud when wanted; do not make literacy a condition of participation.

## Operating this disc during a lesson
The opening menu visibly scans. The highlight moves, a soft cue sounds, and the option is spoken. OK selects the highlighted option. Scene choices and footer controls repeat. After narration, the footer offers Continue, Main menu, and Bookmark.

The four-second interval after an option's speech is fixed in this build. If it is unsuitable, use the person's preferred partner-assisted method at their pace and operate OK on their communicated choice. A missed scanning opportunity is not a comprehension result.

A session bookmark can restore the bookmarked scene and its choices while the session remains active. It is not reliable storage after power-off or eject. Record the set and episode on the staff sheet for later use.

For Set One's continuing story, use **Continue** whenever possible. Direct scene selection loads a matching predefined story state and may not retain the person's earlier route. If jumping into an episode, tell the participant that the story may use an earlier choice they did not make in this session; record the view actually heard.

Each lesson below lists the set, episode, a scene selector number, and an exact opening cue. Numbers are derived from the frozen source's dialogue order, including branches. Verify them on the physical player during the first use. A cue is a place to listen for, not a requirement to skip preceding dialogue.

When you want to move from screen to activity, use the player's Pause if it works in menus. Otherwise agree a break and mute the repeating menu audio, restoring sound before continuing. Do not expect the disc to stop automatically for a printed activity.

## The repeatable staff sequence
1. **Invite:** name the activity and accept the person's decision about joining.
2. **Orient:** show the relevant page and explain what will happen in a short sentence.
3. **Experience:** let the selected passage play; avoid talking over it.
4. **Offer:** ask one meaningful question or offer a real choice, including declining.
5. **Wait and support:** use the person's pace. Offer clarification, a model, or access assistance only as appropriate. Do not keep escalating prompts to obtain an answer.
6. **Connect:** carry out or direct the optional real-world activity.
7. **Record and close:** reflect the person's contribution back, confirm anything ambiguous, ask what should go in the book, and record factual staff observations separately.

## Ways to participate
These are options, not ability levels or a ladder.
- **Experience and indicate:** listen, look or use a description, indicate interest, continue, stop, or a preference through the established communication method.
- **Select and contribute:** choose a photo, direct an action, ask or respond using AAC, match a chosen item, or have someone record a confirmed message.
- **Describe and investigate:** explain, dictate, draw, write, compare sources, or pursue a question.
A person may use different options on different days or within the same session.

Distinguish access assistance from answer prompting. A supporter holding a book or operating a camera can still be carrying out the participant's independently expressed choice. Handwriting or motor independence is not the sole measure of authorship.

## S1E1 — A picture for Haven
**Disc:** Set 1, Episode 1. **Book:** pages 3–4. **Curriculum domains:** RS-1, RS-2, RS-5.

**Objective:** Express a picture preference, including declining, and decide whether a real-world picture may be shared.

**Prepare:** Two familiar-place photographs or a camera; the participant's preferred communication system; removable photo corners.

**Listen for:** Scene 5, n0005: “Asterion, this is Haven research station. Our common room needs something from the outside. Could your Signal Operator send us a picture from your current position?”

**Possible discussion break:** Scene 19, n0020: “We’re off duty now. This is a good time to talk about the picture you chose, what you noticed, and what you might choose next time.”

**Run the activity:**
1. Play from the start of Set One, Episode One. Let the participant hear the request and both options; the disc offers planet or stars.
2. Ask: 'Which picture would you like to send?' Honor the selection or a request to wait. Do not steer toward the picture staff prefer.
3. After Elena invites discussion, ask: 'What place would you like to call our Haven?' Offer a familiar room, a chosen community place, or another idea.
4. Support the participant to choose or direct a photograph. Ask separately whether they want it in their book and whether anyone else may see it.

**Bring it into our world:** Choose a meaningful place and make a photograph, drawing, or spoken description of it. An existing photograph works if going out or taking a new photograph is not wanted.

**Support choices:** Use the person's established indication or partner-assisted selection; read or describe the materials when wanted. Offer a model without treating imitation as an independent answer. The person may direct someone else to handle materials, make a different response, listen, or decline.

**Keep the content accurate:** Haven is a fictional research station. Our Haven is a place the participant chooses; it need not be their residence.

**Observe:** Record the actual options offered, the person's indication, any assistance, and their sharing decision. Staff operating the camera is not evidence that staff chose the subject.

**Close and offer another time:** Invite the participant to choose someone to show the picture to, or choose to keep it private.

## S1E2 — A picture has a story
**Disc:** Set 1, Episode 2. **Book:** pages 5–6. **Curriculum domains:** RS-2, RS-3.

**Objective:** Choose a question or comment about another person's picture and respond in an individually meaningful way.

**Prepare:** A staff or peer photograph offered voluntarily; two question cards; a pass option.

**Listen for:** Scene 4, n0025: “I have seen that picture on your console, but I do not know the story. Where did it come from?”

**Possible discussion break:** Scene 19, n0041: “We will let you get back to it.”

**Run the activity:**
1. Play Elena's question about the picture and Chase's explanation. The disc offers asking about the visit or about the picture.
2. After the selected reply, pause and say: 'We learned something he wanted to share.' Model one brief follow-up connected to the answer.
3. Invite a willing peer or staff member to share a nonprivate photograph. Ask the participant whether they want to ask, comment, listen, or pass.
4. Offer: 'What do you like about it?' or 'What happened there?' Accept a different question. Let both people choose when to end the conversation.

**Bring it into our world:** Have one reciprocal exchange around a willingly shared picture. Participation may be an AAC message, a gesture, a spoken question, or a decision to listen.

**Support choices:** Use the person's established indication or partner-assisted selection; read or describe the materials when wanted. Offer a model without treating imitation as an independent answer. The person may direct someone else to handle materials, make a different response, listen, or decline.

**Keep the content accurate:** Elena models asking and responding; Chase also says when he must return to work. Warm conversation and clear roles can coexist.

**Observe:** Record what the participant communicated and how the partner responded. Do not score looking at the speaker, smiling, or speaking aloud as required evidence of interest.

**Close and offer another time:** Return to the photograph on another day if the person wants to ask something else.

## S1E3 — Choose a view; prepare together
**Disc:** Set 1, Episode 3. **Book:** pages 7–8. **Curriculum domains:** RS-1, RS-2, RS-5.

**Objective:** Choose between two meaningful options and take a chosen role in a short shared preparation.

**Prepare:** Two possible observation locations or photographs; three large preparation cards.

**Listen for:** Scene 1, n0053: “Our connection with Haven is ready. Now we need a view worth sharing. Chase has found two places where the Asterion can make an observation. We have time to hear about both. Operator, your choice will help decide what the people at Haven see when their gathering begins.”

**Possible discussion break:** Scene 22, n0075: “Position confirmed. You helped choose our destination and brought the crew's preparations together.”

**Run the activity:**
1. Play the two observation descriptions. The disc offers planet or stars again; this can change the earlier view.
2. Ask: 'What would you like to spend time noticing?' Accept a changed preference.
3. At the crew readiness reports, name one contribution from the pilot, captain, and operator without making rank a lesson in obedience.
4. Offer a real preparation such as choosing a window, placing the book, or signaling ready. Let the person select a role and ask someone else for any remaining help.

**Bring it into our world:** Prepare a short observation together: choose the place or picture, arrange needed materials, and communicate ready or not yet.

**Support choices:** Use the person's established indication or partner-assisted selection; read or describe the materials when wanted. Offer a model without treating imitation as an independent answer. The person may direct someone else to handle materials, make a different response, listen, or decline.

**Keep the content accurate:** The crew has different jobs. The operator's preference helps direct their shared activity.

**Observe:** Record the chosen role, the actual step completed or directed, and whether 'not yet' or help was communicated. Physical assistance and decision ownership are recorded separately.

**Close and offer another time:** Use the same three-step preparation for another chosen household or community activity.

## S1E4 — Look closely; keep a question
**Disc:** Set 1, Episode 4. **Book:** pages 9–10. **Curriculum domains:** RS-1, RS-4, RS-6.

**Objective:** Indicate one detail of a picture and distinguish an observation from a question with chosen support.

**Prepare:** A familiar photograph; a second enlarged view or paper viewing frame; 'I notice' and 'I wonder' cards.

**Listen for:** Scene 4, n0081: “We have saved the wide picture. Now we can choose a part to explore. You do not need to search for a hidden answer. The display will offer two details from your view. Pick the one you would like to hear more about, and Chase will bring it closer.”

**Possible discussion break:** Scene 22, n0102: “Then our report can include what we noticed and something we still wonder about.”

**Run the activity:**
1. Play the episode on the selected route. Planet offers edge or clouds; stars offers pair or group.
2. After the close-up discussion, ask: 'What can we notice here?' Accept descriptions in any mode.
3. Model: 'I notice this shape. I wonder what is outside the picture.' Do not treat a guess as something the photograph proves.
4. Let the participant select a detail in their Haven picture and an invitation to share it. The disc's message choice is either looking closely or asking what others notice.

**Bring it into our world:** Make a whole-picture and detail pair from the participant's chosen image. Record an observation and an optional question, not a required written answer.

**Support choices:** Use the person's established indication or partner-assisted selection; read or describe the materials when wanted. Offer a model without treating imitation as an independent answer. The person may direct someone else to handle materials, make a different response, listen, or decline.

**Keep the content accurate:** A close view shows a part of the wider picture. One image may not answer questions about motion, distance, or the unseen side.

**Observe:** Record the selected detail and the exact observation or question. Mark a staff-modeled statement as modeled rather than independently generated.

**Close and offer another time:** Find another view or take a later picture if the participant wants to investigate the question.

## S1E5 — Share the observation
**Disc:** Set 1, Episode 5. **Book:** pages 11–12. **Curriculum domains:** RS-2, RS-3, RS-5.

**Objective:** Choose how to share a contribution and acknowledge or decline a response from another person.

**Prepare:** The chosen whole/detail pair; a willing audience; the participant's book.

**Listen for:** Scene 2, n0113: “Everything you prepared is still here: the wide picture, the detail you chose, and your invitation. We will send them in that order. First, Haven gets to see where we were looking. Then we show what you picked out. Finally, they hear the message that goes with it.”

**Possible discussion break:** Scene 16, n0129: “They work together. And your invitation gives us somewhere to begin. We can carry on looking after the ship signs off.”

**Run the activity:**
1. Play the report being sent. Check that the book records the route actually heard, not the unvisited branch.
2. Ask: 'Would you like to show your pictures to someone here?' Accept keeping them private.
3. If agreed, invite the participant to choose 'Look at this detail' or 'What do you notice?' Allow the other person a genuine response.
4. Offer a closing message, another contribution, or a finish. The disc offers thanking Haven or wishing them a good evening.

**Bring it into our world:** Make a small, participant-directed sharing moment with a chosen person. This can happen in the room or with someone elsewhere if the participant wants that.

**Support choices:** Use the person's established indication or partner-assisted selection; read or describe the materials when wanted. Offer a model without treating imitation as an independent answer. The person may direct someone else to handle materials, make a different response, listen, or decline.

**Keep the content accurate:** Haven receives the wider view, detail, and invitation. The story carries earlier choices forward.

**Observe:** Record the invitation, any audience response, and the participant's preferred ending. Do not infer satisfaction from compliance.

**Close and offer another time:** Keep the pictures in a place the participant chooses and revisit only by invitation.

## S2E1 — Earth is our home
**Disc:** Set 2, Episode 1. **Book:** pages 13–14. **Curriculum domains:** RS-1, RS-4, RS-6.

**Objective:** Connect a familiar place with Earth and choose an Earth feature to explore.

**Prepare:** Earth picture; a participant-chosen local photograph; ocean and land labels.

**Listen for:** Scene 5, n0144: “Here is Earth. It is a planet, a large, nearly round world moving around the Sun. From this view, blue oceans cover much of its surface. There are broad areas of land and white clouds above them. Everything in this picture belongs to the same world.”

**Possible discussion break:** Scene 14, n0154: “We have our starting point. Whatever else we discover, Earth will stay marked on our map.”

**Run the activity:**
1. Play the explanation of exploring a map, then the Earth description.
2. Show a familiar picture and say: 'This place is on Earth too.' Invite the participant to indicate Earth or their familiar place.
3. Let the disc's ocean/land choice reflect the participant's interest.
4. Compare the broad Earth view with the local photo. Ask: 'What can we see in this one that is too small in that one?'

**Bring it into our world:** Place the participant's Haven photograph beside the Earth image, connected by a line, an arrow, or a verbal explanation.

**Support choices:** Use the person's established indication or partner-assisted selection; read or describe the materials when wanted. Offer a model without treating imitation as an independent answer. The person may direct someone else to handle materials, make a different response, listen, or decline.

**Keep the content accurate:** The map is changing scale; the ship is not traveling those distances. A room too small to see in the Earth picture still exists.

**Observe:** Record a selection or relationship indicated, including the model or cues provided. Selecting ocean is a preference, not a scored science answer.

**Close and offer another time:** Use a local map or globe to find another place the person chooses.

## S2E2 — The Moon and changing light
**Disc:** Set 2, Episode 2. **Book:** pages 15–16. **Curriculum domains:** RS-4, RS-6.

**Objective:** Explore a model of light on a whole object and indicate a lit or shaded part.

**Prepare:** A large ball and ordinary lamp or flashlight aimed away from eyes, or two prepared photographs; Moon picture.

**Listen for:** Scene 4, n0159: “The Moon travels around Earth. That journey is called an orbit. Gravity helps keep the two connected. The Moon does not stay over one building or one town. It moves around our planet while Earth and the Moon travel around the Sun together.”

**Possible discussion break:** Scene 12, n0167: “Moonlight is sunlight reflected from the Moon's surface. The Moon does not make its own sunlight. And the ordinary changes from crescent to full Moon are not Earth casting a shadow over it. Earth's shadow on the Moon is a different event, called a lunar eclipse.”

**Run the activity:**
1. Play the orbit and surface discussion. This episode has no two-option content branch.
2. After the phase explanation, show a ball with one side lit. Keep the whole ball visible.
3. Change the viewing position or use two photographs. Ask: 'Where is the light now?' The participant may direct the movement instead of moving.
4. Explain that this is a model, not a complete scale copy of Earth, Moon, and Sun. End with the Moon still shown as a whole world.

**Bring it into our world:** Explore light and shadow on the ball or photographs. Offer looking, describing, pointing, or directing the demonstration.

**Support choices:** Use the person's established indication or partner-assisted selection; read or describe the materials when wanted. Offer a model without treating imitation as an independent answer. The person may direct someone else to handle materials, make a different response, listen, or decline.

**Keep the content accurate:** The Moon remains whole. Its phases depend on our view of its sunlit portion; ordinary phases are not Earth's shadow.

**Observe:** Record what the person indicated about light or shade, and whether it followed a model. Do not require memorizing phase names.

**Close and offer another time:** If desired and conditions allow, notice the Moon from a window later; the activity can also use a photograph.

## S2E3 — Different planets; one solar system
**Disc:** Set 2, Episode 3. **Book:** pages 17–18. **Curriculum domains:** RS-1, RS-4, RS-6.

**Objective:** Choose Mars or Saturn and indicate one feature or comparison using accessible materials.

**Prepare:** Earth, Mars, and Saturn images; the solar-system diagram.

**Listen for:** Scene 2, n0172: “Our guide has reached the Sun. It is the star at the center of our solar system. Earth and the other planets orbit it. This diagram spreads the worlds out so we can identify them; it is not a photograph of the planets lined up together.”

**Possible discussion break:** Scene 15, n0186: “The solar system also includes moons, asteroids, comets, and dwarf planets, including Pluto. Our map is a beginning, not a picture of every object.”

**Run the activity:**
1. Play the Sun and planet introduction without requiring recall of all eight names.
2. Let the participant choose Mars or Saturn on the disc.
3. Compare the selected image with Earth. Ask: 'What catches your attention?' Offer red landscape or rings as observations if modeling is useful.
4. Connect the three worlds on the solar-system diagram and clearly name the Sun as the star.

**Bring it into our world:** Create a two-picture comparison: Earth and the selected planet. The participant may choose or dictate a caption.

**Support choices:** Use the person's established indication or partner-assisted selection; read or describe the materials when wanted. Offer a model without treating imitation as an independent answer. The person may direct someone else to handle materials, make a different response, listen, or decline.

**Keep the content accurate:** The Sun is a star. Earth, Mars, and Saturn are planets. The displayed lineup is a diagram, not a photograph of their current positions.

**Observe:** Record the preference separately from any stated feature. Do not count a copied caption as independent identification.

**Close and offer another time:** Look for another chosen comparison in ordinary life: two places with the same category name can still be different.

## S2E4 — Learning with instruments
**Disc:** Set 2, Episode 4. **Book:** pages 19–20. **Curriculum domains:** RS-4, RS-6.

**Objective:** Explore how a tool can provide information that is not clear from a picture alone.

**Prepare:** Heliosphere diagram; an ordinary room thermometer or another familiar measuring tool with an accessible readout.

**Listen for:** Scene 3, n0190: “The Sun sends out more than light. A stream of tiny charged particles flows outward from it. Scientists call that stream the solar wind. It is very different from the air moving through trees on Earth. This wind travels through the space between the planets.”

**Possible discussion break:** Scene 13, n0200: “Right. We can label it, the Sun's surrounding bubble. Beyond it, there is more space to explore. And sunlight does not stop at the outline, either.”

**Run the activity:**
1. Play the solar-wind explanation and identify the outline as part of a diagram.
2. When Haven asks how people know, introduce a familiar measuring tool.
3. Ask: 'What can this tool help us find out?' Demonstrate its real use, with staff reading or describing the output if needed.
4. Return to the spacecraft example: instruments provide measurements. Do not imply the room tool measures space or that invisible means imaginary.

**Bring it into our world:** Take or direct one ordinary measurement, such as room temperature. Record the reading or what the tool told us.

**Support choices:** Use the person's established indication or partner-assisted selection; read or describe the materials when wanted. Offer a model without treating imitation as an independent answer. The person may direct someone else to handle materials, make a different response, listen, or decline.

**Keep the content accurate:** The heliosphere is a region shaped by the solar wind. Its drawn outline is not a wall or the complete outer limit of the solar system.

**Observe:** Record the tool chosen, what the person directed or interpreted, and support used. Staff reading a number is an access support, not evidence of the participant reading it.

**Close and offer another time:** Choose a second everyday question that could be answered with a tool or another source of information.

## S2E5 — Finding home on a larger map
**Disc:** Set 2, Episode 5. **Book:** pages 21–22. **Curriculum domains:** RS-4, RS-5, RS-6.

**Objective:** Use a short picture sequence to connect Earth, the solar system, and the Milky Way.

**Prepare:** Three large cards labeled Earth, Solar system, and Milky Way; a familiar-place picture.

**Listen for:** Scene 5, n0207: “This outside view of the Milky Way is an illustration informed by observations. Nobody has sent a camera far beyond our galaxy to take this portrait. We are inside it, so astronomers put together many kinds of measurements to understand its overall shape.”

**Possible discussion break:** Scene 12, n0214: “Here are the oceans and land again. Somewhere on this world is the place where you are listening. We have changed the scale of our map many times, and we have found our way back to the same home.”

**Run the activity:**
1. Play the explanation of the Milky Way illustration and the Sun's location.
2. Offer the three cards together. Name each at the participant's pace.
3. Follow the spoken return journey with the cards. Ask the participant which card to return to, or invite them to direct the order.
4. Finish at the familiar-place picture: 'We are here on Earth.' Avoid turning the sequence into a speed or memory test.

**Bring it into our world:** Make a foldout or row of cards that travels from home to the larger view and back. Use two cards instead of three if that suits the chosen activity.

**Support choices:** Use the person's established indication or partner-assisted selection; read or describe the materials when wanted. Offer a model without treating imitation as an independent answer. The person may direct someone else to handle materials, make a different response, listen, or decline.

**Keep the content accurate:** The outside view of the Milky Way is an illustration based on evidence, not a photograph taken from outside our galaxy.

**Observe:** Record the relationship or next card indicated, including models. Following the guide with support is different from recalling the sequence independently.

**Close and offer another time:** Use the same change-of-view idea with a room plan and a building picture.

## S3E1 — Other galaxies
**Disc:** Set 3, Episode 1. **Book:** pages 23–24. **Curriculum domains:** RS-1, RS-4, RS-6.

**Objective:** Choose a galaxy view and notice a visible feature without requiring a classification test.

**Prepare:** The spiral and elliptical views from the disc or book; a date-stamped familiar photograph if desired.

**Listen for:** Scene 7, n0225: “One of our large neighbors is Andromeda. Like the Milky Way, it is a spiral galaxy. Its light takes about two and a half million years to reach us. So the light we receive today began its journey long before any person now alive was born.”

**Possible discussion break:** Scene 16, n0235: “Galaxy comparison saved.”

**Run the activity:**
1. Play the explanation of other galaxies and traveling light.
2. Offer the disc's spiral/elliptical choice.
3. After the selected description, ask: 'What do you notice about its shape?' A person may simply indicate the part they want to hear about.
4. Use an older familiar photograph to discuss receiving information about another time. State that the photograph example helps explain an idea; it does not reproduce astronomical light-travel times.

**Bring it into our world:** Choose a galaxy image for the mission log and add a word, mark, or spoken observation.

**Support choices:** Use the person's established indication or partner-assisted selection; read or describe the materials when wanted. Offer a model without treating imitation as an independent answer. The person may direct someone else to handle materials, make a different response, listen, or decline.

**Keep the content accurate:** A galaxy contains many stars. The spiral example on the disc is not an outside photograph of our Milky Way. Far-away light takes time to reach us.

**Observe:** Record what was selected and what was indicated about it. Preference for one image does not establish understanding of galaxy structure.

**Close and offer another time:** Offer a question about the other view if the person wants a comparison.

## S3E2 — What does a map symbol mean?
**Disc:** Set 3, Episode 2. **Book:** pages 25–26. **Curriculum domains:** RS-2, RS-4, RS-6.

**Objective:** Use a label or key to connect a map symbol with what it represents.

**Prepare:** Local Group diagram; a simple map of familiar places using two or three clearly labeled symbols.

**Listen for:** Scene 5, n0241: “Is a group of galaxies the same thing as a group of stars?”

**Possible discussion break:** Scene 12, n0248: “Astronomers combine measurements of distance and motion with their images. Those measurements help reveal which galaxies share a neighborhood and how that neighborhood behaves. Sometimes new measurements change the map.”

**Run the activity:**
1. Play Haven's question about stars and galaxies.
2. Point out the key or labels and name what a marker stands for.
3. Show a separate familiar-place map. Ask: 'What does this mark stand for on this map?' Read the key aloud when needed.
4. Model checking the key instead of guessing from appearance. Explain that a familiar neighborhood comparison is about maps, not the same scale or gravitational relationships.

**Bring it into our world:** Choose a symbol for the participant's Haven and use it on a simple familiar-place map.

**Support choices:** Use the person's established indication or partner-assisted selection; read or describe the materials when wanted. Offer a model without treating imitation as an independent answer. The person may direct someone else to handle materials, make a different response, listen, or decline.

**Keep the content accurate:** A galaxy group contains galaxies; a star cluster contains stars. On the Local Group map, each galaxy marker represents an entire galaxy.

**Observe:** Record whether the person matched, selected, or requested the meaning of a symbol, and the support provided.

**Close and offer another time:** Use an accessible sign or map key at a chosen real destination.

## S3E3 — Different tools; different information
**Disc:** Set 3, Episode 3. **Book:** pages 27–28. **Curriculum domains:** RS-4, RS-6.

**Objective:** Notice that two information sources can tell different things about the same subject.

**Prepare:** Galaxy-cluster image; a familiar object or room picture and a relevant description or measurement.

**Listen for:** Scene 5, n0256: “There is more in a cluster than its galaxies. Very hot, thin gas fills much of the space between them. It gives off X-rays. X-ray telescopes can reveal that gas even when an ordinary visible-light photograph does not show it.”

**Possible discussion break:** Scene 13, n0264: “More like a map that shows relationships at different scales, with different kinds of connections.”

**Run the activity:**
1. Play the cluster explanation. Describe visible content accurately; do not point to an X-ray layer unless one is actually present.
2. After the discussion of instruments, present a familiar picture and another source, such as a spoken description or thermometer reading.
3. Ask: 'What does this source tell us that the picture does not?' Offer a concrete example before inviting a response.
4. Explain that unanswered questions remain. Do not present a decorative color or a schematic as a direct measurement.

**Bring it into our world:** Make a two-part record of one subject: what we can see, and what another source tells us.

**Support choices:** Use the person's established indication or partner-assisted selection; read or describe the materials when wanted. Offer a model without treating imitation as an independent answer. The person may direct someone else to handle materials, make a different response, listen, or decline.

**Keep the content accurate:** The narration discusses X-ray information. The frozen disc's topic image is not proof that an X-ray overlay actually appears. The Local Group is not the Virgo Cluster.

**Observe:** Record the source chosen and the information indicated. Distinguish staff explanation from the participant's own inference.

**Close and offer another time:** Choose an everyday question that requires asking someone or checking a tool rather than looking at a picture.

## S3E4 — Patterns and spaces
**Disc:** Set 3, Episode 4. **Book:** pages 29–30. **Curriculum domains:** RS-1, RS-4, RS-6.

**Objective:** Choose a denser or less crowded region of a model and indicate a pattern.

**Prepare:** Cosmic-web diagram; large removable paper markers or an accessible digital equivalent.

**Listen for:** Scene 3, n0268: “Galaxies do not spread out perfectly evenly. Many lie along long strands, called filaments, and broad sheets. Dense regions form where parts of that pattern meet. Between them are enormous regions with far fewer galaxies, called voids.”

**Possible discussion break:** Scene 12, n0278: “Now let us bring both parts back into view. The strands and the less crowded spaces help us recognize the pattern together. Looking only at the brightest knots would leave out much of the shape.”

**Run the activity:**
1. Play the explanation of strands and voids.
2. Let the participant select filament or void on the disc.
3. Offer large markers arranged with a crowded area and a less crowded area. Ask which area they want to explore.
4. Invite the person to direct one change or describe a pattern. Name this a model of distribution, not a scale picture or a demonstration of all the physics.

**Bring it into our world:** Create a pattern with many markers in one area and fewer in another. The participant can direct placement without handling pieces.

**Support choices:** Use the person's established indication or partner-assisted selection; read or describe the materials when wanted. Offer a model without treating imitation as an independent answer. The person may direct someone else to handle materials, make a different response, listen, or decline.

**Keep the content accurate:** The cosmic web is a pattern of matter distribution. Its drawn strands are not roads or ropes; a void need not be completely empty.

**Observe:** Record the area chosen, pattern indicated, or arrangement directed. Counting every object is not required.

**Close and offer another time:** Look for a pattern in a freely chosen everyday scene without using people's seating or movement as a behavior exercise.

## S3E5 — Questions, discoveries, and home
**Disc:** Set 3, Episode 5. **Book:** pages 31–32. **Curriculum domains:** RS-1, RS-2, RS-5, RS-6.

**Objective:** Choose a discovery or question to keep and decide how to finish or share the mission log.

**Prepare:** The completed or partly completed mission log; Earth picture; participant-selected earlier pages.

**Listen for:** Scene 5, n0287: “Correct. It marks a limit associated with what can reach an observer. It does not show that Earth sits at the center of the whole universe. An observer in a distant galaxy would have an observable region centered on their own location.”

**Possible discussion break:** Scene 17, n0299: “We will leave the final picture at Earth. There is a much larger universe around it, and this is our place within it.”

**Run the activity:**
1. Play the explanation of observation limits without expecting recall of every term.
2. During the return to Earth, follow whichever pictures the participant wants to revisit.
3. Ask: 'What would you like to keep, ask, or share?' A question, a preferred picture, listening, or ending is a valid choice.
4. Invite the participant to choose a future activity or to finish. Let them decide where their mission log will be kept and who may see it.

**Bring it into our world:** Make a closing page with one chosen image, discovery, or question, then connect it back to the participant's Haven.

**Support choices:** Use the person's established indication or partner-assisted selection; read or describe the materials when wanted. Offer a model without treating imitation as an independent answer. The person may direct someone else to handle materials, make a different response, listen, or decline.

**Keep the content accurate:** An observable limit is not a wall around all space. Changing a map's scale is different from the universe expanding.

**Observe:** Record the selected contribution, the person's sharing or privacy decision, and any requested next step. A completed book is not required.

**Close and offer another time:** If requested, plan an accessible library, museum, community, or home follow-up around the participant's interest.

## Reusable closing questions
Offer one, not an interrogation: “Keep going or finish?” “Would you like that in your book?” “Who may see it?” “Anything you want to change?” A confirmed refusal is useful communication and should lead to a changed activity, a break, or an end.
