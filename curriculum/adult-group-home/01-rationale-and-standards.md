# Rift Signal: curriculum rationale and standards crosswalk
Best Polity LLC · Adult group-home edition · Draft 1 · 24 September 2026

## Audience and purpose
This companion curriculum is for adults with developmental disabilities who choose to participate in Rift Signal with staff support in group homes or related adult services. Age, interests, communication, sensory access, movement, literacy, and desired assistance vary. The package does not assign a developmental age, require speech or handwriting, or presume a deficit in interest in other people.

The intended experience is shared exploration: enjoy a story, make consequential choices, encounter accurate science, contribute to a shared task, and connect an experience on the screen to something in the participant's own life. Enjoying or listening to the story can be a chosen activity in its own right. Staff must not make access to it conditional on completing a worksheet.

## What “standards-based” means here
There is no single national school-style curriculum standard that applies to every adult group home. This draft uses national adult-support and communication frameworks as its primary reference, with educational standards as secondary content references. It has not been reviewed against any particular state's waiver, provider, licensing, documentation, or school requirements.

## Curriculum rationale
Rift Signal provides a consistent fictional role, a predictable one-button interaction, recurring crew members, and choices that affect what the story shares. The companion package adds something the disc cannot provide by itself: another person who listens, access accommodations, everyday materials, opportunities to act beyond the screen, and a record that belongs to the participant.

The instructional sequence is **encounter → choose or respond → explore together → connect to life → reflect or finish**. This is a design proposal to support participation, not a claim that the product has demonstrated treatment efficacy.

The first set emphasizes contributing, asking, noticing, preparing, and sharing. The second locates familiar life within Earth and the solar system. The third considers larger structures and the limits of what a picture or model can tell us. A staff member can keep a session brief, repeat it, use only one page, or offer the disc without the book. Completing all fifteen lessons is not a prerequisite for success.

Transfer is made concrete. In the earlier planning discussion, the owner proposed making a picture of “our Haven.” This draft retains that idea while letting the participant choose the place: a room, a residence, a community place, or another meaningful location. The participant can select, direct, describe, or decline a photograph. The resulting record is not simply proof of attendance; it is something they helped author.

## Alignment across the disc
- **Set One:** RS-1 through RS-5 are central. The close-view episode also offers RS-6 through discussion of what a picture does and does not establish.
- **Set Two:** RS-4 and RS-6 provide the science thread. Preferences, communication, and the return to the participant's own place support RS-1, RS-2, and RS-5.
- **Set Three:** RS-4 and RS-6 continue through galaxy comparisons, map keys, information sources, patterns, and observation limits. Sharing or keeping a question supports RS-1, RS-2, and RS-5.
- Each staff lesson names its selected domains and gives one concrete objective. Staff choose whether that objective is relevant for the person; the objective is not an attendance requirement.

## Access and authorship
Use the person's preferred language, communication system, positioning, and individually established response signals. Offer pictures, objects, spoken descriptions, large print, AAC, gestures, pointing, or writing as appropriate. A partner may record or physically carry out a clearly communicated choice. Record that assistance accurately.

The disc's scanning interval is fixed in this frozen version. Repeating options reduce a one-time deadline but do not make that timing suitable for everyone. If direct operation is not accessible, offer partner-assisted selection at the person's pace; the partner then operates the remote for the choice the person communicates. Do not interpret failure to hit the interval as lack of understanding.

Do not require eye contact, a specific facial expression, sitting still, a particular speaking style, or a quick response. Do not treat stillness alone as evidence of comprehension. A participant may decline the book, conversation, photograph, or whole session. A supporter should confirm an ambiguous response using the person's established communication approach rather than inventing its meaning.

Staff may be warm, interested, and supportive while naming their role honestly. The reciprocal-conversation lesson is one opportunity to practice an exchange, not an assertion that adults with disabilities lack interest in others. Neither partner owes private information or continued conversation.

## Observation and documentation
The staff form records an opportunity, the response, the access arrangement, the support used, and a useful next step. It does not calculate a score or declare treatment progress automatically.

Separate:
1. A **preference**, for which either option or declining is acceptable.
2. A **content response**, which can be discussed against the actual picture or model.
3. A **motor action**, which may need assistance even when the choice is independently expressed.

Record “not observed” when appropriate. Do not substitute “failed,” “noncompliant,” or “did not understand” for an unclear, declined, interrupted, or inaccessible opportunity. A useful repeated observation is whether a personally meaningful action occurs under comparable conditions with the support the person chooses—not whether staff can make the person complete more pages.

Example, not a prewritten participant record: “Offered planet, stars, and not now using the person's usual partner-assisted scan. Participant indicated stars with their established yes signal; staff operated OK. Participant then chose a common-room photo for the log and asked to keep it private. Next time, offer taking a new photo if requested.”

Agency documentation remains separate from the participant's own book. The book need not contain disability labels, clinical goals, prompt codes, behavior notes, or private service information. This package supplies activity observations, not a universal billing or regulatory documentation form.

## Content and fidelity limits
The frozen disc is identified by SHA-256:
`276fda2da30c210836a0dbf9b5b6c3b416e2712067f563f1e1b7ac102d5bca18`.

Its reference source checkpoint is commit `391035c3036e2b9cc7536078769faf9fa0d8f99a`. The companion does not alter it.

There are fifteen episodes, not twelve. Lesson titles in this package are companion titles. The published disc is the authority for what actually plays. Staff should follow the participant's chosen branch and not read unvisited branch content as though it occurred.

The narration sometimes describes a close-up, map change, or instrument layer that may not appear as a distinct visual in the frozen topic-based rendering. The staff guide supplies an optional physical activity; it does not claim that an absent visual was shown. In particular, the cluster lesson must not identify the existing Webb image as an X-ray gas overlay.

The participant book uses original, simplified line diagrams labeled as illustrations, not photographs or scale measurements. Blank photograph spaces are intentional places for participant contributions.

## Review and release criteria
Draft 1 requires review with staff and willing adult participants. Check that entry cues match the player, large-print pages are usable, wording is respectful, communication options work for the person, and the real-world activities are wanted. Verify any local service-plan or documentation mapping with the provider before using that mapping. No field trial or outcome study is claimed.
